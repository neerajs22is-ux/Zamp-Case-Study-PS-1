# PS-1 Target Accounts — Bengaluru Tier-1 / Tier-2

> **Purpose:** Name the companies in Bengaluru where invoice processing (PS-1) is a real, painful, high-volume problem — so the case study demos for a specific buyer instead of "any company".
> **Prepared:** October 2026 · **Method:** public sources only (company contact pages, filings, investor pages). No company was contacted.
> **Read the confidence notes before quoting any number.** Revenue is a *volume proxy*, not a measured invoice count.

---

## 1. What makes a company a PS-1 fit

PS-1 hurts when five things are true at once:

| Signal | Why it matters | How to check it |
|---|---|---|
| **Vendor invoice volume** | Automation ROI scales with documents/month | Revenue, number of legal entities, supplier count in annual report |
| **PO-based procurement (P2P)** | Without POs there is nothing to match; the product becomes data entry | Published PO terms, procurement contact, Coupa/SAP Ariba job ads |
| **Multi-entity / multi-GST / multi-currency** | Duplicates, tax mismatches and split POs appear at entity boundaries | Subsidiary list, GST registrations, global operations |
| **Shared-services / AP centre** | A single buyer owns the pain and the budget | GCC "business solutions" scope, AP/P2P job postings in Bengaluru |
| **Audit pressure** | Turns "nice automation" into "must-have controls" | Listed company, BFSI, IPO-bound, Big-4 auditor |

**Scoring:** 5/5 = ideal design partner. 3–4 = good. Under 3 = logo, not a customer.

| 4 | **Bosch India** | Published PO terms = a real P2P process to match against | "Indirect-materials POs with tolerance bands and rate contracts" |
| 5 | **Tesco Bengaluru** | AP is already centralised in the GCC; one buyer, clear budget | "Shared-services invoice backlog and exception routing" |

**How to use this in the case study:** pick **one** of these as the demo persona (e.g. "Zetwerk's AP team, 40k invoices/month") and build the corpus, exception queue and dollar-impact framing around *that* company's reality. A demo aimed at a named buyer beats a generic "enterprise" demo every time.

## 8. Method, confidence, and what to verify next

**Verified:** Bengaluru footprints from company contact pages, exchange filings and corporate sites; Peak XV portfolio membership via `peakxv.com/companies/<slug>` (HTTP 200 for Meesho, Zetwerk, Razorpay); Zamp customers and outcomes from zamp.ai; founder/investor facts from zamp.ai/investors.

**Inferred, not measured:** invoice volume (revenue + segment proxy), AP headcount, ERP stack, and "AP is processed in Bengaluru" for GCCs. No company's invoice count or vendor count was obtained.

**Deliberately excluded after checking:** TVS Motor (regd. office Chennai), LTIMindtree (HQ Mumbai), Shell (finance/AP in Chennai), Standard Chartered GBS (scope unverifiable), Page Industries (contact page dead).

**To harden before quoting in an interview:**
1. Pull each shortlisted company's live **AP/P2P requisitions** (Naukri/LinkedIn) — a Bangalore-based "AP Analyst, SAP" posting is proof the work is here.
2. Pull **trade payables** and **cost of materials** from the latest annual report — converts the revenue proxy into a payables figure.
3. Confirm the **ERP** (SAP ECC/S4, Coupa, Ariba) from job ads — it determines integration effort and therefore deal size.
